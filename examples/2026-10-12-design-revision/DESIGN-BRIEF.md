# Taste recipes for the next render

These directions supplement SYSTEM-INSTRUCTIONS.md. New artwork and fixtures
fork the existing designs; published inputs and prior catalog rows stay intact.

Operator priority: **more expressive art direction and imagery**. This must be
a visible compositional redesign, not merely removing clutter or changing a
colour. Use dramatic scale, strong typography and intentional image placement.
Keep clarity as a constraint, not the entire aesthetic ambition.

## MangaJDM — artwork-led editorial posters

Reader: a car and manga enthusiast viewing a portrait post on a phone.
Takeaway: recognize the specific car study or apparel concept immediately.

1. Give the supplied artwork the largest uninterrupted area. Preserve meaningful
   car details and the whole garment; avoid a universal framed image card.
2. Use a short, specific subject headline before supporting copy. Replace
   generic obsession/chapter slogans with R34, Integra generations, or Silvia
   apparel content. Retain the apparel concept disclosure.
3. Keep brand and destination legible but subordinate. Establish a consistent
   series through alignment, type and palette, with composition suited to each
   asset's proportions.

Use paper, ink and the supplied red accent. Remove the dotted wallpaper,
diagonal corner stripes, heavy end bars and button-shaped call to action.
No new images or fonts are required. Use existing pinned Inter for continuity;
record that intentional choice. No invented products, availability or claims.
Keep margins deliberate, use one spacing scale, and define type and palette
roles in the new fixtures within the existing schema.
Aim for a manga automotive editorial cover: oversized subject type, bold
ink/red contrast, and imagery that owns the canvas. Give the R34, multi-era
Integra artwork and apparel concept distinct compositions within one family.
Use the supplied images themselves; new AI-generated artwork is optional only
if a specific unmet need is established, never a substitute for the brand art.

## Belief updating — a readable feedback diagram

Reader: someone encountering the process for the first time.
Takeaway: consequences feed the next event; examining a belief offers a route
to a revised outlook and further revision.

1. Make the feedback route and its arrowheads the primary visual structure.
   Preserve all semantic connections, especially C1 to A2, B2 to D to E,
   and C2/E to the continuing beliefs.
2. Lead with plain-language node labels. Keep A/B/C/D/E notation secondary,
   still visible for correspondence with the source. Remove duplicated formula
   emphasis and equal-column framing when they compete with the diagram.
3. Make examination and revised outlook one clearly accented branch. Give
   the continuing revision path enough space to read as a continuation.

Use the pinned Barlow Condensed face, larger readable captions, solid fills,
and controlled neutral/accent roles. Remove ornamental icon rings and avoid
arbitrary shape variety. Keep arrows away from text. Use space and alignment
before cards. Preserve the schematic disclosure and source wording; do not
introduce clinical claims or imply guaranteed positive outcomes.
Aim for a memorable explanatory plate: a strong sweeping feedback path,
purposeful visual metaphors for belief and examination, and a distinct accent
branch. Domain-specific imagery is welcome where it explains the node; do not
reduce the redesign to a plain text flowchart or reuse generic icon badges.

Publish new recipe versions through tools/catalog.mjs only. Preserve original
fixtures, artifacts and design records. Render all three posters and the
diagram with both Satori and Chromium through the existing engine. Inspect
each at full size and thumbnail size, checking flow, crop, hierarchy, and fit.
Record AS-rule decisions and honest exceptions in the revised example README.
Keep recipe/fixture changes in one commit and final artifacts/receipts/catalog
design registrations in the next, with reproducible commands and hashes.
